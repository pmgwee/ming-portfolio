# Career Plan evidence — non-tech roles

Research started 19 October 2026; completed 20 October 2026 (Asia/Kuala_Lumpur). Requested filename retained. Scope: the 16 non-tech role IDs in `src/lib/career-plan.ts`. Research only; no application changes. Agent Reach Exa search/fetch, Jina Reader and web search/open were used. No LinkedIn access, private profiles, outreach or job applications.

## How to use this evidence

These sources support particular responsibilities, capabilities or registration requirements, **not** the complete three-step ladders currently displayed. Treat non-regulated paths as illustrative possibilities, not a prediction, prerequisite sequence or guarantee. Evidence strength is claim-specific; do not label every role “verified career path.” Practitioner accounts are examples, not representative labour-market data. No salary, promotion-time or probability estimates are supported here.

## Important corrections before publishing

1. **Remove unsupported precision.** Delete the finance 0–2 / 5–9 / 10+ / 15+ year labels and investment, marketing and creative-director promotion ranges. These sources do not justify them. A regulatory minimum training period must be labelled as such, not mixed with career-speed estimates.
2. **Separate pathways from evidence.** Use “One possible route” and show which capability or requirement each link supports. A source proving that a job needs leadership does not verify the preceding three job titles. Different employers use the same title for different responsibilities.
3. **Medical registration is not simply a qualification.** The MMC 2025 procedures (PDF pp. 1–4 and 7) describe full registration, qualification/training, work experience and competence/character criteria. Applicants have different routes; exceptional/comparable routes have specific conditions. Link readers to MMC for their circumstances. Do not promise recognition for any qualification purely because it is called a Master’s or a parallel pathway.
4. **Do not date the house-officer guide by its URL.** Its foreword is dated **23 April 2008** (printed p. 4); it also tells readers to check updates. Its two-year description is historical corroboration, not sufficient alone to label a 2026 requirement current. Prefer leaving duration out of the demo unless the main research verifies a current MOH/MMC housemanship policy. Specialist requirements above come from the separate 2025 document.
5. **PRP is a minimum plus assessment, not “about a year then automatically registered.”** PBM 2023 guide §§1.1–1.3 and 2.6–2.7 (PDF pp. 2–3) specifies at least a year, eligible premises and satisfactory assessment, with extension possible. This does not validate a “licensed pharmacist” promotion tier, a specific Poisons Act licence for every management job, or the current compulsory-service situation. Verify current PBM requirements directly for the person's pathway; this is career information, not legal advice.
6. **Qualifications differ by sector.** Do not mandate a Master’s for research assistants, a postdoc for industry scientists, ACCA/MIA for every finance-director appointment, or a design degree for every creative-leadership role based on these sources.
7. **Founder/independent work is not a promotion ladder.** Replace inevitable milestones with options or experiments. Brand deals, retainers, hiring, paid ads, marketplace sales and VC funding are choices with trade-offs, not universally necessary achievements.
8. **Avoid unsupported evaluative claims.** The sources do not establish “top 5% without burnout,” an individual role's survival in 5–10 years, or that a job should be accepted/rejected based on five binary answers. Present the check as self-reported fit and questions to investigate, with unknowns visible; salary, wellbeing, caring responsibilities and constraints remain relevant.

## Useful output ideas (editorial suggestions, not source findings)

Show one concrete next step alongside each role, clearly labelled “Try this” rather than an evidence-backed prediction:

- Finance/investment: explain a cash-flow or investment decision, its assumptions and downside risks.
- Consulting/supply chain: map one operational problem, identify the evidence needed, and define a measurable outcome.
- Marketing/creative/UX: present one case study connecting the audience/user need, a creative or design decision, and observed results; show limits rather than invent impact numbers.
- Creator/founder/e-commerce/studio/independent: test a small offer with prospective customers and record costs, delivery constraints and learning; success is not promised.
- Healthcare: check the relevant regulator's pathway and discuss supervision/registration with an authorised training contact; avoid simulated clinical competency judgments.
- Research: compare three real vacancies in the chosen scientific field for techniques, credentials and evidence requested.

## Implementation and validation

The demo maps 20 bilingual goals to scoped source findings, an editorial weekly exercise, a question to ask a practitioner and explicit limitations. Exercises and illustrative routes are not presented as validated predictions. Unknown goals do not borrow another role's evidence.

Removed unsupported promotion durations, automated accept/reject recommendations and claims to predict AI replacement or burnout. Incomplete job checks stay neutral. Both the homepage demo and full tool use the same safer five-question interpretation.

`node scripts/test-career-plan.mjs` checks all 20 evidence profiles, exact English/Chinese role matching, 243 answer combinations in both tools and preservation of existing data during handoff. Desktop/mobile browser checks cover role changes, answer reset, language switching and layout. TypeScript and the production build are release checks.

### Interface revision, 20 October 2026

At the user's request, removed the on-screen evidence accordions and replaced the static advice block with an opportunity map. Its five markers directly represent the visitor's answers: unanswered → Check, no → Build, yes → Use. These are self-reported conditions, not a readiness metric. The next-step focus prioritises a gap, then an unanswered question; visitors can select any area instead. A weekly time budget divides the visitor's chosen 1–8 hours into define/make/review sessions. Role-specific exercises remain grounded in the researched profiles; references remain in the PDF appendix. No external data stream, prediction or generated completion percentage is implied.
